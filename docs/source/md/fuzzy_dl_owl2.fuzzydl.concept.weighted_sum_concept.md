# Summary

A fuzzy description-logic concept that aggregates several sub-concepts into a single weighted sum, blending their membership degrees according to user-supplied floating-point weights.

## Description

`WeightedSumConcept` models a linear mixture of fuzzy concepts, which lets a fuzzy ontology express composite notions such as "mostly cheap and somewhat fast", where each component contributes a proportional share to the overall degree of membership. It derives from the generic `Concept` type together with a weighted-concepts interface, so instances slot directly into the wider concept algebra used by the reasoner. Construction enforces two invariants that are central to the fuzzy semantics: the list of weights must be exactly as long as the list of sub-concepts, and the weights must total no more than 1.0, reflecting the intention that the aggregation behave like a convex-style combination of membership degrees rather than an arbitrary sum. A canonical name of the form *(w-sum (w1 C1) (w2 C2) ...)* is generated automatically, and together with a structural hash computed from the weighted concept pairs, the type name, and the concept type, it gives every instance a stable identity for use in sets and dictionaries.

Instances behave as effectively immutable values: the unary minus, `&`, and `|` operators build fresh concepts by delegating to a shared `OperatorConcept` factory, so weighted sums compose freely with other fuzzy concepts inside larger logical expressions without mutating the operands. Traversal helpers expose the internal structure to the rest of the system, gathering every atomic concept and every role buried anywhere in the nested sub-concepts, and recursively substituting one concept for another throughout the hierarchy (the rebuilt sum is returned under negation, matching the convention applied when substitution happens inside negated expressions). A cloning facility produces a shallow copy whose weight and concept lists are independent of the original, so structural edits to the clone cause no side effects, although the underlying concept objects themselves remain shared between the two instances.
