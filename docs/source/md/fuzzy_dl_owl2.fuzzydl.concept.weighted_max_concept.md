# Summary

A fuzzy description-logic concept that models the **weighted maximum** of several sub-concepts, each paired with its own numerical weight.

## Description

`WeightedMaxConcept` combines the base `Concept` type with the weighted-concepts interface to express the weighted-max aggregation operator used in fuzzy ontologies, where the membership degree of the compound concept is taken as the maximum over the weighted degrees of its constituent sub-concepts. Construction is deliberately strict: the list of weights must be exactly as long as the list of sub-concepts, and at least one weight must equal 1.0 — a condition required for the operator to be semantically well-formed — with any violation reported through the shared error utility. Once validated, the instance derives its canonical textual form, a string such as "(w-max (C1 w1) (C2 w2) ...)", which serves both as its display name and as a component of its structural identity.

The concept integrates into the wider concept algebra by overloading the unary minus, ampersand, and pipe operators for negation, conjunction, and disjunction respectively, each delegating to the shared operator-concept machinery so that weighted maxima compose freely with other compound expressions. Traversal helpers recursively gather the atomic concepts and role names buried anywhere inside the weighted structure, letting reasoners and query processors discover the vocabulary an expression depends on, while a cloning method produces shallow copies backed by fresh lists so the clone's containers can be modified without affecting the original. A recursive replacement facility rewrites occurrences of one sub-concept with another while preserving the original weights, and — as a notable quirk — returns the *negation* of the rebuilt weighted maximum rather than the maximum itself. Hashing is grounded in structural identity, combining the hashes of the sub-concepts paired with their weights, the generated name, and the concept type, so that structurally identical expressions behave consistently as dictionary keys and set members regardless of when or where they were created.
