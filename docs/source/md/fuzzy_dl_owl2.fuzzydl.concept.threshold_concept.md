# Summary

ThresholdConcept wraps an arbitrary fuzzy-logic concept with a numeric cut-off, asserting that the degree to which an individual satisfies the inner concept must be at least (positive threshold) or at most (negative threshold) a given weight.

## Description

ThresholdConcept models graded membership constraints within a fuzzy description-logic framework, enabling expressions such as "an individual belongs to concept C to a degree of at least w" or "at most w". It inherits from the generic Concept hierarchy while mixing in HasConceptInterface to hold and delegate to the wrapped inner concept, so queries about atomic concepts and roles are transparently forwarded to the constrained concept rather than duplicated at the threshold level. Two static factory methods, exposed at module level under the shorter aliases **PosThreshold** and **NegThreshold**, provide ergonomic construction of each threshold direction, and the constructor guards against misuse by asserting that the concept type is one of the two recognised threshold kinds.

The design emphasises composability with the wider concept algebra: operator overloading for unary negation, conjunction, and disjunction builds compound expressions by delegating to OperatorConcept, allowing threshold concepts to be freely combined into larger logical formulas without callers needing to know the underlying representation. Structural manipulation is likewise supported, with cloning producing an independent copy that preserves the type, inner concept, and weight, and recursive replacement rewriting occurrences of a target concept inside the wrapped expression before rebuilding the threshold node around the result with the original weight intact. Human-readable names are computed automatically in the form `([>= w] C)` or `([<= w] C)`, and hashing is derived from the combination of the inner concept, the concept type, and the weight, giving value-based identity semantics that let threshold concepts be used safely and predictably in sets and dictionaries.
